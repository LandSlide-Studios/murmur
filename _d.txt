feat: add words to the dictionary by hand

The recognition machinery was never the problem. Measured on synthesised
speech through the app's own path:

  vocabulary empty    471 ms   "Jim Stikoud"     "Sidence"   "PEDS MD"
  vocabulary seeded   292 ms   "Jim's Steakout"  "Sidance"   "Peds MD"

Hotwords already reach faster-whisper, already fix exactly the mishearings
he reported, and cost nothing -- they are faster, because the decoder
settles sooner once it recognises the term.

The problem was that his vocabulary held four terms, and there was no way
to add a fifth. Learning could only fire when Murmur happened to OBSERVE a
correction, and someone dictating into a chat window does not go back and
edit the text. The Vocabulary panel could forget a term and toggle one; it
could not add one.

So: Vocabulary.add_term() and add_many(), and a box in the panel that takes
a pasted list. A typed term needs no misheard form -- the term alone biases
the decoder, which is what fixes a misheard proper noun, and asking the
user to first guess how Whisper will mangle it would make the feature
useless for the case it exists for. Optional "Right Form = heard as" adds a
substitution rule as well.

A hotword-only row has an empty wrong_form, so apply() skips it: an empty
alternative in the pattern would match at every position and splice the
term across the whole transcript.

Also fixes a defect the tier 4+5 gate reported and I had not yet acted on:
_clean_term's filter ran before its mapping, so \r \n \t all failed the
ord(c) < 32 test and were dropped outright -- the branch mapping them to a
space was dead code, and "Vantage Labs" came back as "VantageLabs", a
hotword the recogniser will never be asked to produce.

552 tests.
